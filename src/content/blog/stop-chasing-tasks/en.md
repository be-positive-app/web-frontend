Most owners know the feeling. You gave the task, you said the deadline, and a week later you are the one asking "what happened to it?". Multiply that by twenty people and chasing becomes your real job.

Chasing is not a character flaw of your team. It is what happens when work lives in chats, in someone's memory and in a spreadsheet nobody opens. Here are five habits that remove it.

## 1. Every task has one owner and one date

"The kitchen should clean the fridge" is not a task. "Rashad cleans the fridge by Friday 18:00" is. One name, one date. If two people own a task, nobody does.

## 2. Tasks go to the phone, not the computer

Your cook, your stylist and your driver are not at a desk. If they have to open a computer to see what to do, they will not. Put the task where they already are: their phone, with a notification.

## 3. "I'm stuck" must cost nothing to say

The most expensive words in a company are the ones nobody says. Make "I'm blocked" a button, with a reason and one line. The manager hears it the same minute, not a week later at the deadline.

## 4. Late work climbs on its own

You should not have to check. A late task should remind the employee, then tell the manager, and only then reach you. If it reaches you, it really is stuck, and your time goes where it matters.

## 5. Done means signed off

"Done" from the person who did it is a promise. "Done" after the manager looked at it is a fact. A one-click sign-off step ends most quality arguments before they start.
